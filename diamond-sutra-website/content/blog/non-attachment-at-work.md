The most common misreading of the Diamond Sutra, once you're past the beginner stage, is treating non-attachment as detachment — as if the goal were to care less.

It isn't. The sutra never asks Subhuti to stop acting, giving, or teaching. It asks him to act "without dwelling" — without needing the act, the outcome, or his own role in it to be fixed and permanent.

## Where this shows up on a Tuesday

You give a direct report tough feedback. It lands badly. The attached version of you needs to have been right, needs the feedback to be vindicated by their later behavior, needs your identity as "a good manager" protected.

The sutra's version of you gave the feedback fully — same care, same honesty — and then let the outcome be whatever it becomes, including being wrong.

## A question, not a conclusion

This week, notice one moment where you're gripping an outcome as proof of who you are. Can you keep the care and loosen the grip?

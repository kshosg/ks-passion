If you've never read a page of Buddhist scripture, the Diamond Sutra is a strange place to start — and also exactly the right one.

It's a short dialogue between the Buddha and his student Subhuti, and its whole engine is one move, repeated from a dozen angles: **whatever you can name, isn't quite it.** "A world" isn't really a fixed "world." "A self" isn't really a fixed "self." The moment you clutch a label as final truth, you've built a small prison and moved in.

## Why a non-Buddhist should care

You don't need a shrine to notice that most of your stress comes from defending a fixed idea — "I'm the reliable one," "this negotiation is a loss," "he always does this." The sutra's method is not to argue you out of those ideas. It's to loosen your grip on any of them being the final word.

## One line to carry this week

> "So you should view this fleeting world — a star at dawn, a bubble in a stream..."

Before you defend your next strong opinion, ask: *am I describing something real, or protecting a label I got attached to?*

That's it. That's the whole practice for this week.
